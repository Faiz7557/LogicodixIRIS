## 1. What happened in the 2026 fire season? Is the "hot issue" premise true?

### Takeaway
Premise CONFIRMED, with one correction: on satellite burned-area and CAMS emissions measures, 2026 exceeds 2019 and trails only 2015, driven by a very strong El Niño (the official series cannot confirm this because it stops at July), but the acute phase is now fading as rain arrives (late Oct to 9 Nov). What stays hot on 9 Nov is the aftermath: emergency status still running in some provinces, ISPA counts still climbing, loss estimates just published (3 Nov), a lawsuit, and a dispute over why the official burned-area figure is about one-ninth of satellite estimates.

### Inferences
- Official Jan-Jul figure vs satellite Jan-Sep is about 9.5x (calc), but periods differ, so this is not like-for-like. MapBiomas Jan-Aug vs SiPongi Jan-Jul is 3.4x (calc).
- On the official series, 2026 cannot yet be ranked against 2019/2023, since it stops at July, before the peak. On satellite data, 2026 Jan-Sep exceeds 2019 by 38%.
- Geographic shift: Papua Selatan and NTT lead by burned area, unlike the Sumatra/Kalimantan-centred baseline. Carbon Brief calls Papua a new fire frontier and says Sumatra had a quiet season as of early Sept.
- Timing for a hackathon: the pitch should frame this as "post-peak evaluation and preparing for the next season", since El Niño extends into early 2027 and the rainy season is forecast to be late and short.

## 3. Haze impact in 2026

### Takeaway
Severe and transboundary. ISPU readings hit "berbahaya" in Kalimantan. Haze-period ISPA passed 400,000 in seven provinces by 2 Nov. Singapore logged 14 straight unhealthy days and Malaysia closed schools for about 2 million students. Modelled mortality runs in the thousands, but there is no official haze death count.

## 4. Economic, health and carbon cost

### Takeaway
2026 estimates range from Rp167 trillion (UIII) to Rp565 trillion (CELIOS), neither official. CAMS shows about 195 MtC so far; its scientist said in late Oct that the season was ahead of 2019 and 2006 and behind only 2015 (a weekly comparison, full-year CAMS values not obtained). No World Bank 2026 or 2023 estimate was found.

### Cited Findings
**Baselines**
- 2015 World Bank: at least US$16.1 billion, 1.9% of GDP — [Quartz](https://qz.com/600732/land-clearing-fires-cost-indonesia-lives-and-16-billion-last-year-and-theyre-starting-again) [SE]; Rp221.4 trillion — [Kompas](https://money.kompas.com/read/2026/10/17/133200826/kerugian-ekonomi-akibat-karhutla-2026-diperkirakan-capai-rp-167-triliun?page=all) [M]
- 2019 World Bank: US$5.2 billion, 0.5% of GDP; about US$157 million direct damage — [Mongabay 2019](https://news.mongabay.com/2019/12/indonesia-fires-cost-nation-5-billion-this-year-world-bank) [SE]

## 6. Official vs independent numbers: why they differ

### Takeaway
Independent estimates are 3 to 9 times the latest official number. The largest cause is timing (official series stops at July), then method (Landsat + ground reports vs Sentinel-2 or burn-scar mapping) and scope (national vs KHG vs "handled" area). Comparisons must match period and definition.
